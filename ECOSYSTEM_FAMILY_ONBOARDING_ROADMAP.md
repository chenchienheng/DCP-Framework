# Ecosystem Family Onboarding — Carrier-Neutral Capability Binding

> Lifecycle: `PUBLIC_RESEARCH_REFERENCE`
> Runtime: `false`
> Authority: `none`

External ecosystems may provide useful capabilities, but connection, onboarding, installation, naming, or roadmap placement does not make them part of Runtime or grant authority.

## Core binding questions
For any external carrier/tool/service/model/workflow surface, resolve only what is materially needed:
- what capability is required by the current need?
- what stable subject/source does it act on?
- who owns meaning and action authority?
- what may be read, transformed, retained, written, shared or deleted?
- what evidence proves the expected effect?
- what are failure, stale-state, cost, quota, privacy and security boundaries?
- can the carrier be replaced, disabled or bypassed without redefining the governed object?
- where does useful delta return, and who reconciles it?

## Historical family examples
Repository, Drive/document store, workflow board, communication/intake, presentation, model/reasoning, calendar/time and similar labels remain useful discovery examples only. They do not define permanent architecture families, ownership windows, coupling faces, schedule hubs or writeback anchors.

## Need-driven rule
Do not onboard a family because it exists or because a roadmap says it is next. Bind a carrier only when an affected need requires its capability and the authority/evidence/return contract is valid.

`Connected != Authorized`
`Installed != Needed`
`Roadmap priority != Current need`
`Carrier family != Architecture layer`
`Capability exists != Solution composed`

## Anti-capture / exit rule
A healthy binding should expose its replacement and exit conditions. If the carrier becomes unavailable, expensive, unsafe, stale, unauthorized or unsuitable, the system should be able to HOLD the affected branch, substitute another legal carrier when possible, or return for adjudication without inventing a second source of truth.

The former P0–P3 onboarding order, fixed window ownership, coupling-face mapping, GitHub writeback priority, Gmail/calendar stabilization sequence and machine-readable family-register plan are retained only in Git history as implementation lineage.
